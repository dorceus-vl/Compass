PRINCE2's seven processes take a project from the first idea to formal closure. They split the work between three levels: the project board directs, the project manager manages, and team managers deliver. The processes are not phases; several run in parallel.

## The seven processes

**Starting up a project.** A short pre-project check that the idea is worth initiating. The executive and the PM are appointed, the board is designed, previous lessons are reviewed, and an outline business case and project brief are written, with a plan for the initiation stage. *Telecom example:* a customer asks for a national network refresh; start-up confirms there is a sponsor, a rough scope and enough reason to spend money on planning.

**Directing a project.** Runs for the whole project and belongs to the board. The board authorises initiation, authorises the project, authorises each stage (or an exception plan), gives ad hoc direction, and authorises closure. It does not run the day-to-day work.

**Initiating a project.** The first management stage, where the foundations are laid: the approaches for risk, quality, change and communication, the project plan, the detailed business case, and the controls, all collected in the project initiation documentation (PID). The board's decision to authorise the project rests on it.

**Controlling a stage.** The PM's daily work inside a stage: authorising work packages, reviewing progress, capturing and assessing issues and risks, reporting highlights to the board, taking corrective action within tolerance, and escalating when a tolerance is forecast to break.

**Managing product delivery.** The team manager's side: accepting a work package, executing it, and delivering the products back with their quality checks done. For a telecom project this is often the vendor or field team, so the work package doubles as a clear instruction: products, quality criteria, tolerances, reporting.

**Managing a stage boundary.** Near the end of each stage the PM plans the next stage, updates the project plan and business case, reports on the stage just ending, and records lessons. The board then decides whether to continue. If a stage goes out of tolerance, this process also produces the exception plan.

**Closing a project.** Confirms the products are accepted and handed over, checks performance against the baselines, reviews benefits achieved so far and plans the rest, records lessons, and recommends closure to the board.

## Stage boundaries

Stage boundaries are PRINCE2's built-in decision points, equivalent to the gates in a stage-gate life cycle. At each one the board asks three questions: is the business case still valid, is the next stage plan credible, and are the risks acceptable? Good places for boundaries on a telecom project: after design and before ordering equipment; after a pilot site and before the rollout; before a major cutover. A stage should be short enough to control and long enough to be worth a board decision.

## Management by exception and tolerances

Tolerances are the permitted deviation around a target before the next level up must be told. They are typically set for time, cost, scope, quality, risk and benefits.

- The corporate or programme level sets project tolerances.
- The board sets stage tolerances for the PM.
- The PM sets work package tolerances for team managers.

Inside tolerance, each level gets on with the work and reports routinely. When a breach is forecast, not only when it has happened, the PM raises an exception report to the board with options and a recommendation. The board may approve an exception plan, change scope, or stop the project early. This is what lets a busy steering committee (comité directeur) stay out of daily detail without losing control.

*Example:* the board sets a stage tolerance of ten working days and a small cost percentage. A vendor slips equipment by three weeks. That breaches time tolerance, so the PM escalates at once with options: pay to expedite, re-sequence sites, or move the go-live.

## In practice

- Write down your current stage, its end date and the decision the board must take at its boundary.
- Agree numeric tolerances for the current stage with your sponsor, in an email you can point to.
- Turn the next vendor task into a written work package with products, quality criteria and reporting.
- If any tolerance is forecast to break, draft the exception report now rather than at the next meeting.
- Add a milestone in Compass for each stage boundary, named after the decision the board takes there.
