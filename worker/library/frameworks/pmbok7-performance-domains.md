title: PMBOK 7: the eight performance domains
section: Frameworks
---
The seventh edition of the PMBOK Guide groups the work of managing a project into eight performance domains. They are not phases and not process groups: all eight are active at once, they overlap, and each one has outcomes you can check. Use them as a health check on a live project, one domain at a time.

## Stakeholders

Covers finding the people and groups affected by the project, understanding what they need and keeping them engaged.

- Going well: the customer, operations and vendors know what is coming and when; objections surface in meetings, not in escalations.
- Not going well: a stakeholder appears late with a veto; approvals stall because nobody knew they were needed.

## Team

Covers forming the team, setting norms, building ownership and leadership across it.

- Going well: people raise problems early, own their actions and cover for each other in a crisis.
- Not going well: work waits for the PM to chase it; the vendor team and the internal team blame each other.

## Development approach and life cycle

Covers choosing predictive, adaptive or hybrid delivery, and the phases and cadence that fit the deliverables.

- Going well: the approach matches the work (fixed design for the network build, iterations for the portal), and phases end in clear decisions.
- Not going well: sprints imposed on work that cannot change once ordered, or a rigid waterfall forced on a product nobody can specify yet.

## Planning

Covers organising and coordinating the work: scope, schedule, budget, resources, communications and procurement, refined as you learn more.

- Going well: the plan is credible, baselined, and updated when reality moves; estimates have stated assumptions.
- Not going well: the schedule (échéancier) is a wish list, never updated, and the team works from a different one.

## Project work

Covers running the work day to day: processes, physical resources, procurement, communications and learning as you go.

- Going well: hand-offs between provisioning, field and vendor are smooth; lessons are captured during the project, not only at the end.
- Not going well: constant firefighting, duplicate effort, equipment arriving at the wrong site.

## Delivery

Covers delivering scope and quality that produce the intended value, and getting deliverables accepted.

- Going well: acceptance criteria are agreed up front; the customer signs off without long punch lists; benefits start on time.
- Not going well: deliverables pass internal tests but fail the customer's; scope creeps in without change requests.

## Measurement

Covers choosing measures, tracking performance and acting on what the numbers show.

- Going well: a small set of measures (milestones hit, defects, budget burn, cycle time) drives decisions; the RAG status has a reason behind it.
- Not going well: reports are green until the week before go-live, then red; nobody acts on the metrics collected.

## Uncertainty

Covers risk, ambiguity, complexity and volatility, both threats and opportunities.

- Going well: the risk register (registre des risques) is reviewed every cycle, each risk has an owner and a response, and reserves exist.
- Not going well: risks are logged once and forgotten; every surprise becomes an issue.

## How the domains connect

A weak domain drags the others down. Poor stakeholder engagement shows up as late scope changes (Delivery), which break the plan (Planning) and stretch the team (Team). When one domain looks unhealthy, look for its cause in a neighbouring one.

## In practice

- Score your project on each domain as green, amber or red in five minutes; write one action for every red.
- Check the RAID log has an owner and a response for every open risk.
- Confirm the customer has agreed acceptance criteria for the next deliverable.
- Replace one vanity metric in your status report with a measure you would act on.
- Ask the copilot to review the project against these eight domains.
