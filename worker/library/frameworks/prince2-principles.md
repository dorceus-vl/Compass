---
title: PRINCE2: the seven principles
section: Frameworks
---
PRINCE2 is a structured project method from the UK, now owned by PeopleCert, with a current edition published in 2023. It rests on seven principles. They are the non-negotiable part: you can tailor themes, processes and documents, but a project that drops a principle is no longer being run the PRINCE2 way. Each principle below has a plain description and what tends to break when it is ignored.

## 1. Continued business justification

There must be a valid reason to do the project at the start, and it must stay valid all the way through. The business case (dossier d'affaires) is rechecked at every stage boundary, not filed after approval.

What breaks: projects keep running after the deal that justified them has shrunk or been cancelled. A customer reduces a 60-site order to 20 and nobody asks whether the dedicated design team still makes sense.

## 2. Learn from experience

Look for lessons before you start, record them as you go and pass them on at the end.

What breaks: the same failure repeats on every project. Installs keep failing because building access was never confirmed, and each new PM discovers this on their own.

## 3. Define roles, responsibilities and relationships

Everyone knows who decides, who delivers and who assures, and the project board (comité de projet) represents the business, the users and the suppliers. The current edition stresses relationships as well as roles.

What breaks: decisions drift, two managers give the field team conflicting orders, or the vendor has no seat where trade-offs are made. Nobody can say who may approve a change.

## 4. Manage by stages

Split the project into management stages and authorise one stage at a time, planning the next one in detail only when you get near it.

What breaks: a single plan covers two years in false detail; the board commits all the money at once and has no natural point to stop or redirect.

## 5. Manage by exception

Each level of management gets tolerances for time, cost, scope, quality, risk and benefits (the current edition also gives weight to sustainability). Within tolerance, the level below gets on with it; a forecast breach is escalated at once.

What breaks: either micromanagement (the sponsor approves every purchase order) or silence (the PM absorbs a six-week slip without telling anyone until go-live).

## 6. Focus on products

Define what is to be delivered, with quality criteria, before planning the work to deliver it. Product descriptions make acceptance objective.

What breaks: activity is tracked but outputs are vague. The team is "90% done" for weeks, and the customer rejects the handover documents because nobody agreed what they should contain.

## 7. Tailor to suit the project

Fit the method to the size, complexity, risk and context of the project, including the organisation's own standards.

What breaks: either "PRINCE2 in name only", with the vocabulary but none of the controls, or a small install buried in full documentation that nobody reads.

## How the principles work together

Stages give the board decision points; business justification tells them what to decide; exception management keeps them out of the detail in between; product focus makes progress measurable. Remove one and the others weaken.

## In practice

- Re-read the business case for your project and write one line on whether it still holds.
- Agree explicit tolerances with your sponsor: how many days and how much money you may absorb before escalating.
- Write a product description for the next deliverable the customer must accept, with quality criteria.
- Search the library's lessons before planning your next stage.
- Check every board role (executive, senior user, senior supplier) has a named person.
