---
title: PRINCE2: the seven themes
section: Frameworks
---
The seven themes describe aspects of project management that need attention throughout the project. Each one answers a question and leans on a set of management products (documents). The 2023 edition calls them practices and adjusts some names, for example "organizing" and "issues"; the content below uses the long-established names, which most teams still recognise.

## Business case: why?

Establishes whether the project is, and stays, worth doing. It holds the reasons, options, expected benefits, costs, timescale, major risks and investment appraisal.

- Management products: the business case, the benefits management approach (how and when benefits will be measured, often after the project ends).
- Telecom angle: benefits are usually revenue retention, new contract value or reduced operating cost; make each one measurable.

## Organisation: who?

Defines the project's management structure and roles. The project board (comité de projet) has three interests: the executive (business), the senior user (those who will use the result) and the senior supplier (those who provide the resources). Below sit the project manager, team managers, project assurance, project support and a change authority.

- Management products: the communication management approach, role descriptions in the project initiation documentation.

## Quality: what?

Defines and checks that products are fit for purpose. Starts from the customer's quality expectations and acceptance criteria and turns them into quality criteria for each product.

- Management products: the project product description, product descriptions, the quality management approach, the quality register (planned and completed quality checks).

## Plans: how, how much, when?

Plans are built from products: identify the products, their dependencies, the activities, estimates and resources. Plans exist at three levels: project plan, stage plan and team plan; an exception plan replaces a plan that has gone out of tolerance.

- Management products: project plan, stage plans, team plans, exception plans, product breakdown and flow.

## Risk: what if?

Identifies, assesses and controls uncertainty, threats and opportunities. Each risk has an owner and, where useful, an actionee who carries out the response.

- Management products: the risk management approach, the risk register.

## Change: what is the impact?

Handles issues (requests for change, off-specifications, problems and concerns) and protects the agreed baselines through configuration management. A change authority may approve changes within a budget delegated by the board.

- Management products: the change control approach, issue register, issue reports, configuration item records, product status account, daily log.
- Telecom angle: a customer adding three sites mid-project is a request for change; a delivered router with the wrong optics is an off-specification.

## Progress: where are we now, and where are we going?

Monitors actual against planned performance, forecasts, and escalates when tolerances are threatened. Reporting flows up: team managers to the PM, the PM to the board.

- Management products: work packages, checkpoint reports, highlight reports, end stage reports, exception reports, end project report, the lessons log and lessons reports.

## How the themes interlock

A change request (Change) is assessed against the business case (Business case), may alter the plan (Plans), introduces new risks (Risk), is approved by the right role (Organisation), is checked when built (Quality) and shows up in the next highlight report (Progress).

## In practice

- Check that your project has a risk register and an issue register, and that they are not the same list.
- Write one product description with quality criteria for a deliverable the customer will sign.
- Name your change authority and the budget it may approve without the board.
- Send a one-page highlight report to your sponsor this week.
- Log three lessons from the last stage while they are fresh.
