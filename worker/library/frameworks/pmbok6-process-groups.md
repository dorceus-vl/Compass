The sixth edition of the PMBOK Guide (2017) describes 49 processes arranged in two ways: by process group (when in the flow of management a process happens) and by knowledge area (what subject it deals with). The five process groups are Initiating, Planning, Executing, Monitoring and Controlling, and Closing. Many organisations, and much of the PMP exam material written before 2021, still use this structure.

The groups are not phases. They describe kinds of management activity, and they repeat inside every phase of a project.

## Initiating

Defines a new project or phase and gets permission to start.

- Main outputs: the project charter (charte de projet), which names the sponsor, authorises the PM and states objectives at a high level; the stakeholder register.
- Telecom example: the sales team has signed a multi-site enterprise network deal; the charter confirms scope, sponsor and target in-service window before any engineering starts.

## Planning

Sets the scope, refines objectives and defines how the work will be done.

- Main outputs: the project management plan with its scope, schedule and cost baselines; the work breakdown structure; subsidiary plans for quality, resources, communications, risk, procurement and stakeholder engagement; project documents such as the risk register and requirements.
- Telecom example: site surveys feed a schedule that sequences fibre builds, equipment orders and installation windows.

## Executing

Does the work defined in the plan and coordinates the people and resources doing it.

- Main outputs: deliverables, work performance data, change requests, issue log updates, selected sellers.
- Telecom example: field crews install equipment, provisioning configures services, the vendor ships routers.

## Monitoring and Controlling

Tracks and reviews progress, finds variances, and manages changes.

- Main outputs: work performance reports, approved or rejected change requests, verified deliverables (quality control), accepted deliverables (scope validation with the customer), forecasts.
- Telecom example: weekly variance against the installation baseline, a change advisory review of a late design change, the customer's acceptance test on each site.

## Closing

Formally finishes the project or phase.

- Main outputs: the final product or service handed over, a final report, closed contracts, archived records and lessons learned (leçons apprises).
- Telecom example: handover to operations with as-built documents, billing confirmed, vendor purchase orders closed.

## How they overlap in a real project

The textbook diagram draws the groups as overlapping waves, and that is how they behave:

- Executing and Monitoring and Controlling run together for most of the project; you are always delivering and checking at the same time.
- Planning never really stops. Rolling-wave planning details the next few weeks while later work stays coarse, and every approved change sends you back into planning.
- In a phased project, Initiating and Closing happen at every phase boundary: you close the design phase and initiate the build phase, usually at a gate.
- Monitoring and Controlling wraps around all the others, including Initiating and Closing.

A network migration might cycle through all five groups once per wave of sites, while the overall project sits in a single long Executing period.

## In practice

- Check that your project has a signed charter; if not, write a one-page version and send it to the sponsor (commanditaire).
- Confirm you have a baseline to measure against: a dated schedule and a budget, saved and not overwritten.
- Make sure every scope change in the last month went through a change request.
- If a phase just ended, hold a short close: what was delivered, what was learned, what carries over.
- Run the status report for the last two weeks and look for variances you have not acted on.
