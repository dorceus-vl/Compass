An issue (problème) is something already going wrong that the project has to deal with: a delivery missed, a test failed, a site refused, a key person gone. Most issues are solved inside the team. The skill is in knowing quickly which ones are not, and moving those to someone with the authority to fix them, before the delay becomes the bigger problem. That move is escalation (escalade), and done well it is a normal tool of management, not an admission of failure.

## Severity: how bad, how fast

Rate each issue on a simple scale agreed with the sponsor at the start, so nobody argues about labels in the middle of a crisis:

| Severity | Meaning | Typical response time |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Stops the project or a customer service, or breaks a contract date or a tolerance | Same day, sponsor informed |
| High | Threatens a milestone or the budget if not resolved within days | Within two working days |
| Medium | Causes delay or extra effort but can be absorbed | Within the week, through the working group |
| Low | Nuisance; fix when convenient | Next review |

Two questions sharpen the rating: what does this cost per day it stays open, and when does it start affecting something the customer sees? An issue that is "medium" today can become critical in a week because the maintenance window is approaching.

## Escalation paths and thresholds

Agree the path before you need it, in the charter or the governance plan:

1. **Inside the team**: workstream lead and PM, solved in the working group.
2. **To the sponsor**: when the fix needs authority, money or people the PM does not have, or when a tolerance is forecast to break.
3. **To the steering committee**: when it needs a decision across groups, a change to scope, date or budget beyond the sponsor's own tolerance, or a call on the customer relationship.
4. **Across organisations**: vendor issues follow the contract's path, usually vendor PM, then account manager, then a named executive. Customer issues go through the account team, never around it.

Thresholds turn "should I escalate?" into a check rather than a judgement call made under stress. Typical examples: a milestone forecast to slip by more than five working days; a cost impact above an agreed amount; any critical-severity issue; an issue with no owner after 48 hours; a vendor that has missed two recovery dates. When an issue crosses a threshold, escalate, even if you think the team can still save it. Tell the sponsor what you are doing about it at the same time.

## Escalating well

- **Warn before you escalate.** Tell the person whose area you are escalating about, so nobody is ambushed.
- **Escalate the problem, not the person.**
- **Bring options**, not just the problem.
- **Ask for something specific**: a decision, a resource, a phone call to the vendor's executive.
- **Set a deadline** that matches the impact.
- **Close the loop.** When it is resolved, say so to everyone who heard about it.

## Writing an escalation note

Keep it to one screen. A shape that works:

- **Subject**: the ask and the deadline. "Decision needed by Thursday: approve a second install crew for wave 3."
- **What happened**: two or three sentences of facts, with dates.
- **Impact**: on milestones, cost, the customer, in numbers where possible. Say what happens if nothing is done and when.
- **What we have tried**: so the reader knows this is not the first step.
- **Options**: two or three, each with cost, time and risk.
- **Recommendation and ask**: which option, and exactly what you need from the reader, by when.

Example, in brief: the vendor has missed the equipment delivery for 18 sites by two weeks; go-live for the customer's head office slips from the 14th to the 28th unless we act; we have asked for a recovery date twice; options are to wait, to source 6 units from another distributor at $12,000 extra to protect head office, or to resequence waves; recommendation: the second, with approval needed by Wednesday to hold the installer's slot.

## In Compass

Log issues in the RAID tab with impact, owner, response and due date, and use "Make a task" for the actions. Record the escalation's outcome in Decisions. On a project that allows cloud AI, Athena's "Escalation note" preset drafts the ask, the impact, the deadline and the options for you to edit.

## In practice

- Agree severity levels and escalation thresholds with your sponsor and put them in writing.
- Check every open issue has an owner, a response and a date; escalate any without an owner after 48 hours.
- Write down the vendor's escalation path from the contract, with names.
- Rewrite your last escalation email using the structure above and compare.
- Close the loop on any issue you escalated that has since been resolved.
