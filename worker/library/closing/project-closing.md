Projects rarely end cleanly. The last sites go live, the team drifts to new work, a few defects linger, the vendor's final invoice arrives months later, and nobody is quite sure whether the project is still open. Closing on purpose (clôture du projet) protects the customer, the budget and your reputation, and it frees people properly for what comes next. On most projects it takes two to four weeks of deliberate work.

## Acceptance

Closing starts with confirming the project delivered what was agreed:

- **Customer acceptance**: the customer signs that the service or deliverables meet the acceptance criteria. Get it in writing, referencing the contract or statement of work. On B2B work this often starts billing and the service term, so the account team needs the date.
- **Internal acceptance**: the sponsor confirms the objectives in the charter are met, or records which are not and why.
- **Open items**: anything accepted with reservations goes on a punch list with an owner, a date and a home after the project ends.

If acceptance is stalling, find out why. Usually there is a real defect, or a missing document, or the customer is using acceptance as leverage on an unrelated issue. Each needs a different answer.

## Handover to operations

The service must keep running after the project team leaves. A good handover (transfert à l'exploitation) means operations accepts responsibility knowingly, not by default:

- **Documentation**: as-built records, network diagrams, configurations, IP plans, circuit IDs, vendor contacts, support contracts and their expiry dates.
- **Inventory and systems**: every site, device and circuit recorded correctly in the inventory, monitoring and billing systems. Mismatches here become incidents and billing disputes for years.
- **Support model**: who the customer calls, escalation paths, SLAs, maintenance windows, warranty terms.
- **Known issues**: open defects and workarounds, transferred formally with owners.
- **Training and early support**: a period, often two to four weeks, where the project team backs up operations for questions.

Ask operations to sign the handover. This is often the evidence for the final gate, Gate 5 in [[project-lifecycle]].

## Administrative closure

The paperwork nobody enjoys and everybody needs:

- **Contracts**: confirm every vendor deliverable is accepted, final invoices received and paid, holdbacks released or claimed, and purchase orders closed.
- **Finance**: reconcile the budget, close cost codes so no more charges land on the project, and record the final cost against the baseline.
- **Records**: archive the project documents where the organisation keeps them, with decisions, approvals and acceptance signatures easy to find.
- **People**: release team members formally, tell their managers, and give feedback on their contribution.
- **Benefits**: hand the business case's benefits to the person who will measure them after the project, with the measures and dates.
- **Lessons**: hold the closing lessons session (see [[lessons-learned]]).

## Celebrating

Mark the end. A team lunch, a short note from the sponsor naming individuals and what they did, a message to the customer's team thanking them. It costs little and people remember it at the start of the next project, when you need them again. Thank the vendor's people too; they were part of the team. Recognition works best when it is specific: "the weekend cutover went cleanly because Marc rebuilt the rollback plan on Friday" lands far better than "thanks, everyone".

## In Compass

Tick the final milestones done, record acceptance and handover sign-offs in Decisions, close or transfer the open RAID items, and capture lessons in the Lessons tab. The lessons learned template builds a closing document from the project. Then set the project's status to closed: it leaves the portfolio's active list and moves under "Closed".

## In practice

- Write the closing checklist for your nearest project end: acceptance, handover, contracts, finance, people, lessons.
- Ask operations what they need in the handover pack, before you build it.
- Check with finance which cost codes are still open on finished projects.
- Book the lessons session and the team celebration now, before people move on.
- Draft the thank-you note to the team, naming what each person did.
