Once the contract is signed, the vendor becomes part of your delivery team without being under your authority. You cannot assign their people or set their priorities; you can only use the contract, the relationship and the information you share. Vendor management (gestion des fournisseurs) is the work of turning a signed agreement into dependable delivery, and of noticing early when it is not happening.

## Kick-off

Hold a proper kick-off with each significant vendor, separate from the customer kick-off. Cover:

- **The goal and the customer**: what the project is for, what matters to the customer, what would embarrass everyone.
- **The contract in plain terms**: scope, exclusions, milestones, acceptance, service levels, the change process. Read it together; their delivery team may never have seen it.
- **People**: named contacts on both sides, who decides what, and the escalation path with names (see [[issues-and-escalation]]).
- **Ways of working**: status format and timing, meeting rhythm, tools, how site access and logistics are arranged, safety and security rules.
- **Known risks and dependencies**, in both directions: what you need from them, and what they need from Bell and the customer.

Send a summary afterwards and treat it as the start of the record.

## Cadence

- **Weekly** working session with the vendor's PM: progress against the plan, the next two weeks, blockers, risks, actions from both sides.
- **Monthly** or quarterly service review with the vendor's account manager: the scorecard, trends, commercial matters, improvement actions.
- **Daily** during intense periods such as install waves or cutovers.

Ask for status in a format you can compare week to week, ideally against the same milestones you report to your own sponsor.

## Service levels

Service level agreements (ententes de niveau de service, ENS) turn expectations into measurable commitments: equipment delivered within so many days of order, installs completed within the booked window, defects fixed within a time based on severity, reports delivered by a day each month. Good SLAs measure what the customer actually feels, and come with a clear measurement method and a remedy, such as service credits, an improvement plan, or, ultimately, the right to terminate.

Know which SLAs are in your vendor's contract and which are in Bell's contract with the customer. Gaps between the two, such as a vendor fix time longer than the time Bell promised the customer, are risks for the RAID log.

## Scorecards

A short scorecard (fiche d'évaluation) reviewed monthly keeps the conversation factual. Pick five to eight measures:

| Area | Example measure |
|---|---|
| Schedule | Milestones met on the agreed date, percentage |
| Quality | Sites passing acceptance at the first visit |
| Responsiveness | Time to answer escalations |
| Documentation | As-built records delivered with each site |
| Safety and compliance | Incidents, missed access rules |
| Commercial | Invoices accurate first time |
| Collaboration | Risks raised early by the vendor |

Show the trend, not only the month. Share the scorecard with the vendor before the review, so the meeting is about causes and actions, not about disputing the numbers.

## Handling slippage

When a vendor starts missing dates:

1. **Notice early.** Compare their forecasts with what actually happened, and challenge an unchanged forecast after a missed week.
2. **Understand the cause.** Capacity, supply, a dependency on Bell or the customer, or a real misunderstanding of scope? Some causes are yours to fix.
3. **Ask for a recovery plan** in writing, with dates, resources and checkpoints. Agree how you will track it, often more often than usual.
4. **Protect the critical path.** Resequence work, ask for a second crew, or source parts elsewhere, within the contract's terms.
5. **Record it.** Missed dates, recovery commitments and their outcomes, for the scorecard and for any later commercial discussion.

## Escalating with a vendor

Follow the contract's path, step by step: their PM, then their account or delivery manager, then a named executive. Warn your counterpart before you go above them. Bring facts, the impact, and a clear ask. Involve Bell procurement or vendor management before any commercial remedy such as withholding payment or invoking penalties; those are their tools, and using them badly damages a relationship you may need for years. A firm, fair, well-documented escalation often earns more respect from a vendor than months of tolerance.

## In Compass

Put each vendor commitment on the board as a task in the Waiting on column with the vendor's name, so waiting-on nudges, the weekly review and overdue alerts catch it. Log vendor risks and dependencies in the RAID log, and record recovery commitments and remedies in Decisions.

## In practice

- Read your main vendor's SLAs next to the customer's, and log any gap as a risk.
- Draft a six-line scorecard and send it to the vendor before your next review.
- Check that every open vendor action is on the board under Waiting on, with a due date.
- Write down the vendor's escalation path with names and phone numbers.
- Ask for a written recovery plan on any milestone the vendor has already missed.
