The business case and the charter are the two founding documents of a project. They are often confused or merged, but they answer different questions. The business case (dossier d'affaires) asks: should we invest in this at all? The charter (charte de projet) says: this project now exists, here is its mandate, and this person runs it.

## The business case

The business case justifies the investment and is used to keep checking it.

What it holds:

- The problem or opportunity, and why now.
- Options considered, including doing nothing, with a recommendation.
- Expected benefits, stated so they can be measured (revenue protected, new contract value, cost avoided, churn reduced), and when they arrive.
- Costs: one-time and recurring, internal and vendor.
- Timescale and major risks.
- Financial appraisal as your organisation requires it (payback, net present value, margin).

Who owns and signs: the sponsor or executive owns it; finance often reviews it; the investment committee or steering committee approves it. The PM usually helps write it and keeps it current, but does not own the benefits.

On B2B telecom work, the business case is often tied to a customer deal: the sales case says the contract is profitable; the delivery business case must show it can be delivered at the planned cost and margin.

## The project charter

The charter formally authorises the project and gives the PM authority to use resources.

What it holds:

- Purpose and objectives, linked to the business case.
- High-level scope: what is in and, just as important, what is out.
- Key deliverables and success criteria.
- Milestones at summary level and a target end date.
- Summary budget.
- Key stakeholders, the sponsor, and the PM with their level of authority.
- Main assumptions, constraints, risks and dependencies.
- Governance: who decides, how changes are approved, how often the steering committee meets.

Who signs: the sponsor (commanditaire), and often the heads of the groups that supply the people. On customer-facing projects, the customer may sign a parallel kickoff document or the statement of work rather than the internal charter.

## How they differ

| | Business case | Charter |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Is it worth doing? | Who does what, with which authority? |
| Owner | Sponsor | Sponsor, issued to the PM |
| Focus | Value, cost, options, benefits | Scope, objectives, governance, authority |
| Life | Revisited at every gate and after closure for benefits | Mostly fixed once signed; changed through change control |
| Audience | Investment decision-makers | Project team and stakeholders |

The business case comes first, or at least alongside. A charter without a business case authorises work nobody has justified.

## What a weak charter looks like

- Objectives that are activities ("install equipment at 40 sites") rather than outcomes ("40 sites live on the new service by March, with billing active").
- No "out of scope" list, so every customer request becomes an argument.
- Success criteria nobody can measure.
- No named sponsor, or a sponsor who has not read it.
- The PM's authority left unstated: can they approve overtime, move a site wave, sign a vendor change?
- Governance missing: no steering committee, no escalation path, no change process.
- Assumptions hidden, such as building access, customer readiness or a dependency on another team's release.
- Written once for the kickoff deck and never looked at again.

## Keeping them alive in Compass

The project record holds the core of a charter: objectives, in scope, out of scope, success criteria, sponsor, phase, dates and budget. Fill those in at the start, log the charter's sign-off in Decisions, put its main assumptions and risks in the RAID log, and recheck the business case at each gate milestone.

## In practice

- Read your project's charter and rewrite any activity-style objective as an outcome.
- Add three items to the project's out-of-scope field that the customer is likely to ask for.
- Confirm in writing what you may approve without the sponsor.
- Ask the sponsor which benefits the business case promised, and who will measure them after closure.
- Log every charter assumption in the RAID log with an owner.
