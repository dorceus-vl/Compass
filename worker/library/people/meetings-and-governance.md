Governance is the answer to one question: when something needs deciding, who decides, where, and how fast? Meetings are where most of those answers happen. Too little governance and decisions stall or get made in corridors; too much and the project spends its energy feeding committees. The aim is a small set of forums, each with a clear job, and a written trail of what they decided.

## The usual structure

**Steering committee (comité directeur).** The sponsor and the senior people whose budgets, teams or customers the project touches; on a customer project, often a customer executive too. Its job is direction and decisions above the PM's authority: approving gates, releasing contingency, settling priority conflicts between groups, deciding on changes beyond tolerance. It is not a status meeting. If the steering committee spends its hour hearing what happened last week, the pack should have done that job.

**Project working group or core team.** The PM with the workstream leads (design, network, installs, IT integration, operations, the vendor's PM). Its job is coordination: dependencies between workstreams, next two weeks, blockers, risks. This is where most problems are solved before anyone needs to escalate.

**Workstream meetings and stand-ups.** Owned by the workstream leads, at whatever rhythm the work needs. The PM attends selectively.

**Customer meetings.** A regular slot with the customer's project contact, plus wider reviews at milestones. Keep internal debates out of these; settle them first in the working group.

**Technical or change boards.** Change advisory boards for maintenance windows, architecture review boards for design decisions. These belong to the organisation; your job is to know their deadlines and get your items onto their agendas in time.

## Cadence

| Forum | Typical rhythm | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Steering committee | Every 2 to 4 weeks, plus at gates | 45 to 60 minutes |
| Working group | Weekly | 45 to 60 minutes |
| Customer check-in | Weekly or every two weeks | 30 minutes |
| Cutover calls | Daily or several per day during the window | 15 minutes |

Increase the rhythm in critical periods and say so; reduce it when things are stable. A meeting that has had no decisions or actions for three weeks running should be shortened or stopped.

## Agendas that lead to decisions

Send the agenda (ordre du jour) with the pack at least a day ahead, two for the steering committee. For each item, state what you want from it: "for decision", "for input" or "for information". Put decisions first, while people are fresh, and information last, or in the pack only.

A steering committee agenda that works:

1. Decisions required (with options and a recommendation each).
2. Overall status in one slide: RAG, trend, key milestones.
3. Top risks and issues that need the committee's help.
4. Changes for approval.
5. Next gate and what it will need.

## Minutes, decisions and actions

Minutes (procès-verbal, or compte rendu for less formal meetings) do not need to retell the discussion. They need three things:

- **Decisions**: what was decided, by whom, with any conditions.
- **Actions**: what, who, by when. One owner per action, a real date.
- **Points to follow up**: open questions, with who will bring the answer back.

Send them within 24 hours, while memories agree. Ask for corrections by a deadline; silence after that means accepted.

Decisions and actions must not live only in meeting notes, where they get lost. Keep a decision log (registre des décisions) with date, decision, who decided and the reason, and track actions where the team already looks: the task board, not a separate spreadsheet.

## In Compass

Each project has a Decisions tab: date, text, and a link back when a decision came from a meeting review. When you accept a meeting review into a project, its decisions land there, and you can mark open questions and notes as risks or issues for the RAID log. Actions become tasks on the board. Compass can also produce minutes from a reviewed meeting in Word or Markdown. Gate outcomes belong in Decisions too.

## In practice

- Write one sentence describing the job of each recurring meeting on your project; stop or merge any that do not have one.
- Rebuild your next steering committee agenda with decisions first, each marked "for decision" with a recommendation.
- Check that every action from the last working group has one owner and a date, and is on the board.
- Log the last three decisions that only exist in email into the project's Decisions tab.
- Send minutes within 24 hours for every meeting you chair this week.
