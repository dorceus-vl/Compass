Most project communication problems are not about writing well. They come from sending the right message to the wrong people, at the wrong moment, in a format nobody reads. A communication plan (plan de communication) fixes that by deciding in advance who needs what, how often, through which channel and in which language. It takes an hour to draft and saves weeks of "why wasn't I told?".

## Start from the audience

Work from the stakeholder register (see [[stakeholder-management]]), and ask three questions about each group:

1. What decision or action do they need to take, and what do they need to know to take it?
2. What would they be upset to learn late?
3. How do they prefer to receive information, and in which language?

An executive sponsor needs the RAG, the trend and any decision required, on one page. A customer's network team needs the cutover schedule and the method of procedure. Field technicians need site addresses, access contacts and times. Sending all three the same 12-page report serves none of them.

## The plan's columns

| Column | Example |
|---|---|
| Audience | Customer IT director |
| Purpose | Confirm readiness, surface blockers early |
| Content | Progress against milestones, next two weeks, their open actions |
| Format | Short written summary plus a 30-minute call |
| Channel | Email and Teams call |
| Frequency and timing | Weekly, Tuesday morning |
| Language | French, documents bilingual |
| Owner | PM |
| Feedback | Questions logged as actions; read receipts not required |

Add a row for each audience, including the internal ones the plan is most likely to forget: operations, finance and the account team.

## Cadence

Match the rhythm to the pace of decisions, not to habit:

- **Daily** during cutover windows and incident-heavy periods: short, factual, at a fixed time.
- **Weekly** for the core team and the customer's working contacts.
- **Every two to four weeks** for the steering committee (comité directeur), with the pack sent two working days ahead.
- **At milestones and gates** for wider audiences: a short note when something they care about is done.
- **On exception**, at once, whenever a tolerance is forecast to break. Do not wait for the next scheduled report.

Fix the day and the time. A status that arrives every Thursday by noon builds trust; one that arrives "most weeks" invites people to chase you.

## Channels

- **Email**: for anything that must be traceable, such as decisions, approvals and formal notices. Write the subject line as the message: "Decision needed by Friday: second install crew for wave 3".
- **Chat (Teams)**: quick coordination; never the only record of a decision. Copy decisions into the decision log.
- **Meetings**: for discussion, alignment and difficult news. Not for reading a status report aloud.
- **Shared documents and dashboards**: for reference material people pull when they need it.
- **Phone**: for bad news to a sponsor, before it appears in writing.

## Bilingual audiences (EN / FR)

Many Bell customers and teams in Québec work in French, and some audiences expect both languages. A few habits make this manageable:

- Ask each stakeholder their preferred language at kickoff and record it in the plan; do not guess from a name or a city.
- For mixed audiences, decide upfront whether documents are bilingual side by side, French first, or issued in two versions. Be consistent.
- Keep a short project glossary of agreed terms, such as cutover (bascule), change request (demande de changement) and steering committee (comité directeur), so translations stay the same from report to report.
- Budget time for translation and review of formal documents; it belongs in the schedule, not squeezed in the night before.
- In meetings, let people speak in the language they are most precise in, and summarise decisions in both.
- Check legal or contractual obligations about the language of customer documents with the account team rather than assuming.

## In practice

- Build or update the communication plan for your project, one row per audience, and check that operations and finance have a row.
- Fix a day and time for your weekly status and tell the recipients.
- Ask your two most important customer contacts which language and format they prefer.
- Rewrite the subject line of your next three project emails so they carry the message.
- Start a ten-term bilingual glossary for the project.
